# Elevance Health Nine-Factor Stock Screen

## Valuation context

This screening uses September 1, 2026, as the valuation reference date and ELV's August 31, 2026, closing price of $392.54 as the reference price. The analysis incorporates information available through ELV's second-quarter 2026 reporting. [Source: [StockAnalysis.com, ELV historical data for August 31, 2026](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/elv/history/), accessed September 3, 2026.]

## 1. Business quality — Pass

Elevance Health primarily sells health insurance and benefit-administration services to individuals, employers, Medicare and Medicaid beneficiaries, and federal employees. CarelonRx provides pharmacy-benefit management, specialty-pharmacy, home-delivery, and infusion services. Carelon Services provides behavioral-health management, care navigation, home and post-acute care, utilization management, payment integrity, and healthcare analytics.

This diversification allows ELV to earn revenue from insurance premiums, administrative fees, and healthcare services. However, the company remains substantially exposed to government programs: approximately 32% of its 2025 consolidated revenue came from U.S. government agencies. Overall, ELV passes this factor because it possesses considerable scale and multiple related revenue sources. [Source: [ELV 2025 Form 10-K, Item 1, pp. 3–10](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000013/elv-20251231.htm). Assessment: screening judgment.]

## 2. Valuation — Mixed

At the reference price of $392.54 and management's minimum 2026 adjusted-EPS guidance of $27.00, ELV trades at approximately 14.54 times forward adjusted earnings. This figure is calculated as $392.54 divided by $27.00.

That valuation appears moderate for a large health insurer, but it is not automatically inexpensive. The adjusted-EPS figure excludes certain items included under GAAP, while ELV continues to face medical-cost, membership, and margin pressure. ELV therefore receives a mixed valuation assessment rather than an outright pass. [Sources: [StockAnalysis.com, ELV historical data for August 31, 2026](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/elv/history/); [ELV Q2 2026 earnings release, July 15, 2026, pp. 1 and 13–14](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000058/a2q2026elvearningsrelease.htm). Calculation: $392.54 ÷ $27.00 = 14.54. Assessment: screening judgment.]

## 3. Earnings and cash flow — Pass

ELV reported second-quarter 2026 operating revenue of $49.826 billion, an increase of 0.8% from the prior-year quarter. Diluted EPS was $6.71, while adjusted diluted EPS was $7.45.

Management raised its full-year guidance to at least $20.10 of GAAP diluted EPS, at least $27.00 of adjusted diluted EPS, and at least $6.0 billion of operating cash flow. These figures demonstrate positive earnings and cash generation, although the guidance remains a management forecast rather than a guaranteed result. ELV passes this factor based on its current earnings and cash-flow outlook. [Source: [ELV Q2 2026 earnings release, July 15, 2026, pp. 1–2 and 8](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000058/a2q2026elvearningsrelease.htm). Assessment: screening judgment.]

## 4. Profitability trend — Fail

ELV's second-quarter 2026 benefit-expense ratio increased by 80 basis points to 89.7%. This means that a greater percentage of premium revenue was required to pay members' healthcare costs. The company's operating margin also declined from 4.9% in the second quarter of 2025 to 3.5% in the second quarter of 2026.

Management attributed the higher benefit-expense ratio primarily to elevated medical-cost trends in its government businesses, partially offset by improved performance in the Individual ACA business. Because medical costs are rising faster than the company's current pricing and efficiency improvements can fully offset them, ELV fails this factor. [Source: [ELV Q2 2026 earnings release, July 15, 2026, p. 2](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000058/a2q2026elvearningsrelease.htm). Assessment: screening judgment.]

## 5. Membership trend — Fail

Total medical membership declined by 672,000, or 1.5%, year over year to 44.949 million as of June 30, 2026. Medicare Advantage membership declined by 15.9%, Medicaid membership declined by 4.3%, and Employer Group risk-based membership declined by 5.5%.

These losses were partially offset by growth in fee-based employer membership. Nevertheless, declining risk-based membership can reduce premium revenue and operating leverage. ELV fails this factor because membership is contracting in several economically important categories. [Source: [ELV Q2 2026 earnings release, July 15, 2026, p. 7](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000058/a2q2026elvearningsrelease.htm); [ELV Q2 2026 Form 10-Q, Item 2, p. 40](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000060/elv-20260630.htm). Assessment: screening judgment.]

## 6. Balance sheet and liquidity — Mixed

ELV reported $10.232 billion of consolidated cash and cash equivalents and $31.044 billion of total long-term debt as of June 30, 2026. Subtracting cash from long-term debt produces simplified net debt of approximately $20.812 billion.

Only $2.062 billion of cash, cash equivalents, and investments was held at the parent company and available for general corporate purposes. Insurance regulations restrict how much cash ELV's subsidiaries can transfer to the parent company. Consequently, consolidated cash should not be treated as entirely available for acquisitions, debt repayment, dividends, or repurchases.

ELV receives a mixed assessment because it has meaningful liquidity but also substantial debt and regulatory restrictions on the movement of cash. [Source: [ELV Q2 2026 Form 10-Q, Item 1, pp. 3 and 24–25, and Item 2, p. 49](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000060/elv-20260630.htm). Calculation: $31.044 billion − $10.232 billion = $20.812 billion. Assessment: screening judgment.]

## 7. Capital allocation — Pass

During the first half of 2026, ELV repurchased 4.4 million shares for $1.358 billion at an average price of $310.89 per share. Approximately $5.337 billion of repurchase authorization remained as of June 30, 2026. In July 2026, the company also declared a quarterly dividend of $1.72 per share.

Repurchases can increase each remaining shareholder's percentage ownership, although the ultimate value created depends on whether management purchases shares below their intrinsic value. ELV passes this factor because management is returning capital through both repurchases and dividends while retaining substantial repurchase authorization. [Source: [ELV Q2 2026 Form 10-Q, Item 1, Note 12, p. 29](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000060/elv-20260630.htm); [ELV Q2 2026 earnings release, July 15, 2026, pp. 2 and 4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000058/a2q2026elvearningsrelease.htm). Assessment: screening judgment.]

## 8. Insider buying — Pass

CEO Gail Boudreaux purchased 2,725 ELV shares through two open-market transactions on July 17, 2026. She purchased 680 shares at a weighted-average price of $366.41 and 2,045 shares at a weighted-average price of $368.25, for a calculated total investment of $1,002,230.05.

Director Ramiro Peru purchased 1,000 shares at $366.05 per share on the same date, for a total investment of $366,050. The transaction code "P" on both Form 4 filings identifies these transactions as purchases rather than shares automatically awarded as compensation.

Insider buying does not prove that the stock is undervalued. Nevertheless, it demonstrates that two company insiders were willing to invest personal capital at prevailing market prices. ELV passes this factor based on the size and open-market nature of the purchases. [Sources: [Gail Boudreaux SEC Form 4, Table I, filed July 17, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000128792026000006/xslF345X03/wk-form4_1784310641.xml); [Ramiro Peru SEC Form 4, Table I, filed July 17, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000119204526000004/xslF345X06/wk-form4_1784315092.xml). Purchase totals calculated from the reported shares and prices. Assessment: screening judgment.]

## 9. Risk and recovery potential — Mixed

ELV's principal risk is its ability to forecast and price medical costs correctly. Commercial and Medicaid prices are generally established before the coverage period begins, while Medicare bids are submitted months in advance. If medical utilization, provider prices, or prescription-drug costs exceed the company's estimates, ELV generally cannot recover the difference through higher premiums during the existing contract year.

Additional risks include Medicare risk-adjustment audits, pharmacy-benefit-manager regulation, cybersecurity incidents, and unfavorable changes to ELV's Blue Cross and Blue Shield licensing agreements. CarelonRx also depends on CVS Caremark for important pharmacy services under an agreement whose current term extends through December 31, 2027.

Management is raising premiums and investing in medical-cost management, technology, and operating efficiency. However, the financial benefits of these actions have not yet been demonstrated consistently. ELV therefore receives a mixed assessment for risk and recovery potential. [Source: [ELV 2025 Form 10-K, Items 1 and 1A, pp. 9 and 24–38](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000013/elv-20251231.htm); [ELV Q2 2026 earnings release, July 15, 2026, pp. 1–3](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156039/000115603926000058/a2q2026elvearningsrelease.htm). Assessment: screening judgment.]

## Final screening conclusion

ELV passes the business-quality, earnings-and-cash-flow, capital-allocation, and insider-buying factors. It receives mixed assessments for valuation, balance-sheet strength, and risk-recovery potential. It fails the profitability and membership factors.

**Final call: Watch-defer.** ELV's valuation and long-term business quality are interesting, but the current evidence does not establish that premium increases and Carelon growth can overcome elevated medical costs, membership losses, and margin compression.
